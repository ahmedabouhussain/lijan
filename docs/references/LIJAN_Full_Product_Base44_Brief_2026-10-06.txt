2. ONE PRODUCT, THREE OPERATING MODES

National federation: independent institutions retain ownership and access boundaries. A national secretariat may maintain authorized registry metadata, shared templates, policy standards and reporting obligations. Oversight has specifically granted indicators, extracts and drill-through rights, not universal visibility. A cross-institution committee can have a different constituting authority, appointing authority, secretariat custodian, ratifier and execution owner. It is not necessarily a child of one organization's tree.

Institutional domain: one institution configures its committee portfolio, departments, authorities, policies, templates, membership, reporting and services. Here “domain” means the institution's governed workspace/tenant, not merely an email suffix, URL or SSO connection. Branding, custom domains and identity-provider configuration are separate capabilities; none supplies committee authority.

Holding group: represent parent/subsidiary relationships, reserved matters, delegated powers, subsidiary committees, group committees and reporting requirements. Parent ownership does not automatically reveal confidential subsidiary deliberations. Group reporting and approval rights must be explicit, with source-linked evidence and exceptions. Do not force independent agencies into a holding-company hierarchy.

Personal workspace: the same person can chair one committee, serve as secretary in another, be a member elsewhere and act as vice-chair under a bounded mandate. Employment, represented institution, membership, administrative access and decision authority remain separate. A personal consolidated view does not consolidate institutional ownership. A national committee seat does not create national administrator access. These are configurable operating modes/editions of one product and governed data model, not three forked applications.

4A. COMPLETE COMMITTEE AND MEMBERSHIP LIFECYCLE CONTRACT

Apply this contract across all six streams. These are required product behaviors and acceptance scenarios, not a claim that every jurisdiction uses identical rules. Configure the governing instrument/policy, effective dates, authorized decision-maker and exceptions. Do not hard-code a generic corporate rule as national law. Where the source instrument is ambiguous, show the unresolved policy choice and block only the affected official action.

A. Committee relationships, working groups and delegated work

Provide a “Structure and Relationships” view showing parent/subcommittee, advisory working group, joint committee, appointing body, ratifier, reporting recipient, oversight body and execution dependency as distinct typed relationships. A committee may participate in more than one permitted relationship; the model must extend beyond an organization tree. Show each relationship's legal/mandate basis, scope, owner, start/end dates and current status. Keep canonical committee and work-group identities through naming changes.

A work group may have an assigned remit, outputs, leader, members, timetable, budget/limit where applicable, reporting route and escalation route. Members can come from the parent committee or permitted external institutions; group membership must not silently create parent-committee membership, votes or access. Preserve existing Workstream and WorkstreamMembership foundations and map their actual behavior before creating replacements. Distinguish a formally constituted subcommittee with its own decision powers from an advisory group producing recommendations.

The constituting policy determines what the parent delegates, reserves, ratifies, reviews or can overturn, and whether an appeal/reconsideration route exists. Do not assume every parent can override every child, or that a reporting relationship conveys approval power. Require lawful scope and time limits for delegation; reject unauthorized re-delegation, expired mandates, invalid cycles and powers exceeding the delegator's own authority. Model permitted reporting networks without confusing them with forbidden authority/dependency loops.

Connect parent agenda topics to commissioned child/group work, deliverables, recommendations, dependencies and due dates. A recommendation can be accepted, returned, escalated or incorporated into a parent decision with preserved provenance; it is not itself the parent's approved decision. The same person serving on parent and child must disclose relevant interests and act under the correct capacity. Sharing a deliverable shares only the authorized version/extract, not all working papers or discussions. Show cross-group duplication, mandate overlap and incompatible recommendations as explainable issues for an authorized human to resolve. Preview the effect of delaying or moving an agenda item across forums, linked to the governing mandate and only permitted downstream records; a graph or keyword overlap alone is not proof of jurisdiction.

Acceptance: a parent commissions two subcommittees and an advisory group, including external experts. One subcommittee has bounded delegated authority; the other requires ratification. The advisory group submits a recommendation but cannot issue the parent's decision. Parent oversight receives the permitted report while restricted group papers remain denied. Reject an out-of-scope approval, expired delegation, forbidden authority loop and guessed-ID access across sibling groups. A joint committee's reports to two authorized bodies preserve one source record.

B. Seats, appointments, vacancies and acting capacities

Represent the seat, its represented institution, the person occupying it, appointment/acceptance, committee/group role, term, decision rights and source authority separately. Support one person in multiple authorized capacities without accidental duplicate voting. Define seat allocation, weighted votes, alternates/proxies, attendance and quorum from the governing policy. Multiple represented interests never automatically multiply a person's vote.

Provide “Appointments and Continuity” screens for nominations, acceptance, renewal, end of term, voluntary resignation, removal, death, incapacity, employment/representation transfer, temporary absence and acting appointments. Treat these as different events with effective time, competent authority, reason classification and required approval/evidence. Private personal reasons must not be exposed through ordinary portfolio reporting. Topic-specific recusal temporarily restricts the affected matter; it does not automatically suspend unrelated work or replace the chair permanently. Chair stepping down from the chair role may retain ordinary membership if the instrument permits; secretary departure, vice-chair succession and member replacement need their own valid authority. Do not assume the vice-chair automatically acquires every chair power.

An ended appointment creates a visible vacancy or approved successor/acting assignment. Identify which powers stop, which obligations remain assigned to the institution/seat, whether quorum is affected, and which operations can continue. A new chair/member receives a new assignment under their own identity, not the departing person's account, signature, vote or blanket permission set. Temporary substitute/acting rights have a bounded scope and expiry and cannot silently become permanent. Rejoining later creates a new tenure linked to history, not a resurrection of expired grants.

Acceptance: resign a member, remove only a chair role while retaining membership, appoint a time-limited acting chair, expire it, and appoint a permanent successor. Correct future actions are allowed or denied in each interval. The original person's historical actions remain attributed to them. A vacant required chair position or missing quorum blocks only the policy-dependent activity and offers an authorized resolution path.

C. Controlled handover and institutional history

Deliver a “Handover Pack” and acceptance workflow. Inventory open agenda items, pending approvals/signatures, obligations, upcoming meetings, delegated powers, group memberships, official correspondence, document custodianship, risks, deadlines and unresolved exceptions. Identify the outgoing seat/role, incoming appointment, approving authority, item owner, permitted history scope and effective date. Allow partial handover, return for missing information and reassignment to an interim accountable custodian. Departure must not require a former member to log in before the institution can continue through an authorized process.

Transfer institutional responsibilities and the historical records needed for the successor's mandate, subject to classification and access policy. Preserve original authorship, votes, signatures, approvals, recusals, audit entries and appointment periods. Keep the predecessor's private notes, unrelated memberships, credentials, personal messages and personal delegations out of automatic handover. Do not transfer a prior member's consent or declaration of interests to the successor. Require the successor's own acceptance/declarations where policy requires them.

Reassess every outgoing grant, delegation, alternate and working-group role: expire, revoke, retain or reassign only through the authorized policy. Revalidate recipients of reminders, calendar items and pending approval requests; do not send successor documents to the former occupant. Apply current read policy to historical records separately from the prohibition on future writes. Update search, AI retrieval, caches, citation links, server-side downloads and active sessions accordingly. Retention of a record does not mean continued access for its former holder. Previously downloaded files cannot be assumed remotely recalled; record that limitation and applicable controls.

Acceptance: replace a chair with active subcommittee duties and overdue actions. The approved handover assigns each obligation once, gives the successor only permitted institutional history, and records their acceptance. The successor can understand why an old decision was made but cannot change the former chair's vote or signature. An old browser tab, reminder link, AI answer or export endpoint must not bypass the predecessor's new access state. Private notes and unrelated committee history stay private. Unaccepted handover items remain visible as unresolved, not silently completed.

D. Meetings and decisions during organizational change

For resignation, recusal, removal, vacancy, delegation expiry or a changed appointment during agenda preparation, a live meeting, an open vote, minutes review or pending ratification, apply an explicit effective-time policy. Show affected attendance, eligible seats, quorum, pending votes, signatures and next steps to the secretary/chair. Use authorized choices such as pause, replace prospectively, re-establish quorum, close/reopen a round, reconstitute the meeting or seek ratification. Do not invent a universal automatic cancellation rule.

Freeze the approved proposal, material versions, applicable policy and relevant historical authority facts for each official action/round. A valid vote cast before an effective departure must not be reassigned to the successor or silently erased. A successor cannot recast it as the same historical person. If the governing rule invalidates a round or requires a new vote, create an explicit reasoned supersession/new round and preserve the original. Changes in membership do not automatically invalidate otherwise valid past decisions.

Distinguish effective date from recorded date. Correct erroneous dates/content through traceable, authorized amendment; prohibit silent backdating, fabricated past attendance or retrospective signatures. Withdrawal, reconsideration, appeal, annulment and correction must have separate policy-defined powers and consequences, including downstream obligations already performed. A corrected decision must identify affected execution owners and required follow-up rather than silently changing their source.

Acceptance: expire a delegation during an open vote; replace a signatory during minutes review; discover an appointment-date error after issuance. Demonstrate the configured allow/deny and recovery paths, preserved attribution and understandable user notices. Show both a prior valid decision that remains valid and a separately authorized supersession that updates future execution without deleting history.

E. Committee change, reorganization and orderly closure

Support renewal/reconstitution, rename, mandate amendment, temporary suspension, expiry, dissolution, merger and split as explicit lifecycle operations with an impact preview, competent approval, effective time and linked instruments. Renewing a committee does not automatically renew every seat or silently amend past terms. Preserve term/formation versions and show which appointments and delegations need fresh approval. Organizational merger, department transfer or holding-structure change must not collapse data boundaries or transfer all records by ownership label alone.

Before changing a committee, enumerate affected child committees/groups, appointments, agenda items, votes, records, pending signatures, execution obligations, dependencies, shared templates, reporting obligations and access grants. Require an explicit destination or disposition for each open institutional responsibility. A committee merger/split links predecessor and successor committees and moves selected future responsibilities while retaining source records and original jurisdiction. Prevent duplicated decisions/obligations and orphaned work. A suspended/dissolved committee cannot issue new decisions unless a narrowly authorized wind-down power permits it.

Provide an archive/closure view with outstanding obligations, approved custodian, retained history, access policy, retention schedule and legal holds. A closed committee may still have enforceable execution obligations assigned to a successor/custodian. Archive, restrict access, retain and destroy are different actions. Legal holds override ordinary disposal; no irreversible deletion or retention-policy bypass is authorized by this brief. Expiry must not masquerade as successful completion.

Acceptance: renew a committee with one non-renewed member; suspend a parent with active groups; merge two committees with an overdue shared obligation; split a mandate between successors; dissolve a group whose deliverable is still pending. Demonstrate approved disposition, preserved provenance, no duplicate assignment, correct access and a retained unresolved-obligation report. A legal hold prevents disposal while ordinary viewers remain appropriately restricted.

Cover overlapping mandates/terms, mutually incompatible delegations, duplicate people/appointments/imported records, wrong represented institution, unaccepted invitations, unavailable ratifiers, conflicts discovered late, disputed evidence, returned/withdrawn submissions, partial handovers, overdue dependencies and concurrent changes. An import must validate a dry-run mapping and reject/quarantine ambiguity; never merge distinct people merely because names or emails look similar. Test every sensitive route through the UI and server: direct record IDs, files/downloads, search snippets, counts, exports, AI, notifications, scheduled jobs and multiple tabs. A selected active organization or national/group title is not security authority. Show audit evidence for changes, denied actions and sensitive access within the actual logging design. An error must not become an empty successful list, a false zero, a completed handover or verified closure.

The catalogue must identify policy variations, current coverage and remaining unknowns. Do not claim that a finite test list covers every real-world event. New findings join the same acceptance map and product journeys without repeatedly restarting delivery or replacing the complete product scope.
